# UX Work: evidence brief for the interactive redesign

Status: **Awaiting firsthand detail**. This is a content intake document, not a claim that the proposed scenes or outcomes actually occurred.

## Confirmed, preserve
- Portfolio narrative: recognize relationships between tools, people, and information, then improve the actual work.
- IDL Worldwide, Design Engineer, Apple fixture rollout for AT&T retail, May 2015–May 2017.
- Per-drawing turnaround: initial 8 hours; team improvement 2 hours; later individual refinement 37 minutes.
- More than 500 locations digitized. Do not apply per-drawing savings to each location or imply measured financial, stress, or autonomy gains.
- Collaboration: SolidWorks techniques, coworker shortcuts, encoding inputs across teams, better handoffs, QC, shared learning.
- Earth Hero: prototype, moderated testing, selected recommendations taken into live app; findings confidential.
- Letter River: working prototype; learning outcome not measured.

## Owner writing prompts (answer as fragments, stories, examples, or rough notes)

### A. IDL: the original 8-hour task (essential)
1. What did the request look like when it arrived? Who supplied it, in what form (files, drawings, specs, messages)?
2. Walk through **one representative drawing** from arrival to approval. Which steps took effort, where were interruptions, re-entry, waiting, checking, or unclear responsibility?
3. Which real example best shows the original process working against the people doing the job?
4. If you have a genuine artifact (redacted input, drawing, checklist, screenshot), identify it. Do not recreate an artifact and present it as original.

### B. IDL: the changes (essential)
5. **SolidWorks:** What specific feature, operation, or reusable technique did you discover or use? What did it replace?
6. **Coworkers:** What shortcut or insight did someone else contribute? How did the team discover/share it?
7. **Information preparation:** What exactly was encoded differently, by whom, and how did it change the next handoff?
8. **QC:** What did you change about checking? How did the revised checking process work?
9. What tradeoff, failed attempt, or obstacle forced you to revise the approach?

### C. IDL: outcome and attribution (essential)
10. How were 8h, 2h, and 37m observed or measured? Same kind of drawing? Typical times or isolated examples?
11. What specifically happened between the 2h team workflow and your later 37m workflow?
12. What changed in people's actual day-to-day experience, **without presenting unmeasured effects as proven**? Give one observable example.
13. Which details are confidential or should not appear publicly?

### D. Earth Hero (short)
14. What user behavior or research observation motivated the quest / team-progress designs? Can you share it publicly?
15. What alternative design was considered, and why did you choose the prototype shown?
16. Precisely which recommended elements entered the live product? What remains unverified or confidential?

### E. Letter River (short)
17. What difficulty from learning Hebrew prompted the drifting-letter interaction?
18. Why moving letters into sound targets rather than conventional flashcards or multiple-choice questions?
19. What has been observed in actual play, and what remains a hypothesis?

## Editorial plan once answered
- Opening: show the IDL turnaround result immediately, with team and individual stages identified.
- IDL: a static-at-first, inspectable input → production → handoff → QC system diagram. Highlight real causal connections supported by the answers. Avoid fictional rework quantities, cost, and invented before/after screenshots.
- Interaction: selecting a real intervention updates *both* the affected stage and its downstream dependency, instead of just changing a paragraph. Provide a complete static reading path.
- Evidence: the turnaround and its qualification remain visible near the diagram. Detailed methodology is optional.
- Earth Hero: present the user need → design decision → evidence/adoption. Link to actual prototype.
- Letter River: show interaction and explain the learning hypothesis with clear testing status.
- Verify build, internal navigation, keyboard/focus states, responsive layout, reduced motion, and screen-reader labeling before requesting merge.

## Design rule
An interactive element must reveal a meaningful new relationship, consequence, or decision. Otherwise use static content.
