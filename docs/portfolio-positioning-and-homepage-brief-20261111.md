# Portfolio positioning and homepage brief — 2026-11-11

Status: **strategy and interaction brief, not a visual implementation**.
Scope: `flimmbark-source/Jmeyportfolio`, branch `ux/system-story-evidence-pass-20261104`.
Decision authority: latest user conversation (Nov 11), then verified current branch, then historical briefs. This document proposes the next homepage iteration without silently overriding the earlier relational/experiential interaction grammar.

## 1. Decisions supported by the latest conversation

**Purpose:** Help people better understand themselves and one another. The user prioritizes creating conditions in which people can arrive at understanding for themselves, not transmitting a pre-decided interpretation.

**Method:** UX is a foundational discipline: observe what people actually do and experience, form insights, prototype, test, and iterate. Intuitive or embodied experience can motivate hypotheses, but claims about other people and product outcomes still require verification.

**Practice:** Build digital products, interactive tools, games, and research experiences. Serious and thoughtful first, inventive and playful on closer inspection. Avoid presenting creative exploration as an unrelated hobby.

**Career direction:** Prefer work creating new interactive experiences for learning and understanding, while remaining credible for conventional product/UX roles. Use CET's Product Builder – EdTech and Technical Product Builder vacancies as concrete tests, **not** as the entire identity.

**Current evidence:** Letter River is a working public learning prototype, with no measured learning outcomes. Get to the Café is publicly playable, with an evaluative study not yet conducted. Earth Hero has user research/design work and selected ideas incorporated into the product, with confidential research results. IDL has a retrospective 8h baseline, 2h team stage, and later 37m individual process, not a controlled experiment. These must not be flattened into equivalent claims of impact.

## 2. Primary audience and hiring test

**Primary initial test:** A product builder hiring manager at CET. The November 11 CET openings emphasize recognizing educational problems, taking initiative, using AI tools to make working prototypes, combining product/UX judgment with implementation, collaborating with education and engineering experts, and owning iteration and launch. The Technical role additionally requires deep engineering expertise and strong Python/JavaScript with production shipping evidence. Do not imply the latter are established if not verified.

**Secondary audiences:** UX/product hiring teams and organizations exploring games, learning, disability awareness, and family communication. These should see the same underlying person, not a different portfolio identity.

The opening must answer in one scan:
1. What does this person do?
2. What is unusual or valuable about their approach?
3. Can I see something built and understand the evidence for it?
4. Where do I go to assess collaboration, production work, and technical competence?

## 3. Positioning and proposed homepage voice

**Professional descriptor:** `UX-led Product Designer & Builder`

**Hero headline (recommended draft):**
> I build interactive products around how people learn, work, and understand one another.

**Supporting copy:**
> I start by studying what people actually experience. Then I design, prototype, and test tools and games that help people discover something for themselves.

**Short human note for a deeper layer, not the hero:**
> Sometimes the useful answer is a better workflow. Sometimes it's a game that lets you feel a problem instead of only reading about it.

This leads with understandable work, keeps the deeper philosophy present, and avoids presenting personal spiritual conviction or untested therapeutic claims as evidence. It is a copy recommendation, not approved final language.

Tone: thoughtful, human, candid about uncertainty, precise about outcomes. Not a corporate UX cliché; not a philosophical manifesto; not a claim that every project has caused transformation.

## 4. Project hierarchy for the first visit

This is **attention order**, not an exclusive category system. Existing conceptual connections remain valid and accessible.

1. **Letter River — learning by doing.** Lead for CET relevance: an independently built, usable language-learning product. Primary action `Try Letter River` (external link, not iframe: current known CSP/headers block framing). Show one authentic artifact, the interaction proposition, and the current lack of learning outcome measurement. More technical details available on its case-study route.
2. **Get to the Café — understanding another experience.** The clearest embodiment of the mission. Primary action `Play the game`; secondary `Read the research question`. Make the difference between anecdotal player response, proposed study, and established result explicit.
3. **Earth Hero — collaboration and applied product judgment.** A strong professional counterbalance: research, prototypes, partnership, and selected recommendations entering a live product. Do not invent confidential findings or attribute all product delivery to one person.
4. **IDL workflow — system improvement with concrete outcome.** Show the original audit-to-drawing process, what changed (shared blocks, patterns, configurable assemblies), team versus individual contribution, and the qualified 8h → 2h → 37m figures. This proves the method extends beyond games.

The other games and unfinished work stay discoverable in the relational playground/workshop. They do not all receive equal homepage emphasis merely because they exist.

## 5. Homepage information and interaction architecture

**A. Entry (immediate, without a forced choice).** Show identity, purpose, and a visible real artifact or interaction. No initial “UX or Play” fork as the required path. The fork is a current implementation, not an immutable rule. Visitors should not need to decide whether a project is “professional” to see it.

**B. Featured interactive proof.** Give the visitor an optional, low-friction way to encounter a working artifact. Do **not** force a timed intro, play session, or long animation before the hiring evidence becomes visible. A brief interaction should reveal something meaningful about the product, not be decorative.

**C. Other featured work.** Each project has a compact, consistent information stack: `human need / question` → `what I built or changed` → `what is actually known` → `try / inspect`. The artifact comes before the long explanation. Keep full case studies optional.

**D. Method through demonstration.** Instead of generic “discover → define → design” steps, show an inspectable design decision: user/context evidence, resulting intervention, changed interaction or workflow, and status of verification. A click or focus should update the corresponding artifact or its consequences. If it only reveals more prose, use plain text.

**E. Extended exploration.** Keep the relational canvas, projects linked by intention/concept, explicit Unfinished workshop, preserved classic `/ux`, Research, and Contact. Make it easy to move from a project to its related question and back. The graph is a deeper mode of understanding the work, not the price of entry.

**F. Recruiter shortcut.** “Selected product work”, “UX work”, “Resume”, “Contact” remain directly reachable. Direct URLs, keyboard operation, touch, reduced motion, and a readable static path are mandatory.

## 6. Interaction principles that must survive implementation

- **Experience first, evidence always nearby.** Use the actual build or artifact whenever feasible. Evidence labels distinguish shipped, publicly playable, tested, in-study, hypothesis, and reconstruction.
- **Selection changes meaning, not just decoration.** A lens should reorganize genuine project relationships; selecting a project should privilege the artifact itself. Keep the semantics already documented in `portfolio-v2-interaction-grammar.md`.
- **No false binaries.** The same project can demonstrate research, interaction, engineering, learning, and imagination. Do not split “serious UX” from “play” as two different people.
- **Agency before theatrics.** No mandatory intro/gate, scroll trap, hidden navigation, or hover-only task. The user can browse, play, or go directly to a case study.
- **Honest claims.** Design goals are not product outcomes. Exploratory feedback is not a published finding. Reconstruction is labeled. Confidential results are not made up.
- **Professional usefulness.** A hiring manager can understand role, contribution, collaboration, product state, and evidence without playing a whole game. A curious visitor can go deeper through interaction.

## 7. Initial acceptance tests before visual polish

Recruit 3–5 first-time viewers (ideally one product/UX hiring person, one builder, and one person outside design). Use the initial desktop and mobile path. Ask, without explaining the site:

1. Within 10 seconds, what do you think I do?
2. Which project looks most relevant to learning/product building and why?
3. Find a working project and try or open it.
4. Find one verified result, and identify one outcome that is **not** yet established.
5. Find my professional collaboration/workflow evidence, then return to the experiential portfolio.
6. Can you explain one relationship between projects that the interface surfaced?

Record actual misreadings and failures, not just aesthetic preferences. If people confuse game experiments with validated interventions, or cannot find professional work quickly, revise the information architecture before polishing motion.
